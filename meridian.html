04 — The atelier

“We spend eleven days finishing what no eye will ever see.”

Élise Marchand — Chef d’atelier

Every bridge edge is filed to a 45° bevel and polished with gentian-wood pegs until the metal holds a line of light — inside corners included, especially inside corners.

The plates are stamped with overlapping pearls of boxwood and diamond paste, one rotation at a time. Eight hundred to a plate, none exactly alike.

Reserved for steel: the surface is worked on tin until it reflects only what is placed before it. Under the loupe, a mirror; in the hand, nothing at all.

One watchmaker, one watch, eleven days. The calibre is regulated in five positions and against six extremes of heat before the case back is closed.
