N° 03 — Les Éphémérides

A hundred and fifty years,
counted by hand.

From a rented bench to the same bench, owned.

Archives 1874 · Plaque Argentique · Rue du Rhône, Genève
Tirage d'Époque · Chambre Noire

“Émile Léman seated at the Rue du Rhône bench, where the very first movement was adjusted by the northern lake light.”

Conservé aux archives de la manufacture.
  1. 1874

    Émile Léman, twenty-six, rents a bench above a bookbinder on the Rue du Rhône and finishes his first movement under another man's name.

  2. 1903

    The atelier releases its first calibre under its own signature. Eleven are made. Nine are known to survive; the house pretends not to know where the tenth went.

  3. 1926

    The Répétition strikes the hours on two gongs tuned to the bell of Saint-Pierre. It was said you could set your watch by the cathedral; now, the reverse.

  4. 1948

    The workshop reopens after the war with four hands where there had been forty. The waiting list begins and, in one sense, never ends.

  5. 1969

    Quartz arrives. The house declines, in a polite letter, to convert. The letter is framed; it hangs beside the lathe.

  6. 1994

    A Léman returns to the bench. The founder's great-granddaughter reopens the enamel workshop, and the kiln resumes its ancient habit of saying no.

  7. Today

    312 watches a year. Never more. The waiting list is measured in years and, we find, rarely resented.
